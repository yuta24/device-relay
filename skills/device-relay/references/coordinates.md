# Coordinates and orientation

- Coordinates are in points, with the origin at the top left of the screen in the foreground app's displayed orientation. `screen` in `relay health` is 402×874 in portrait, for example, and 874×402 in landscape.
- A full-resolution screenshot's pixels are `screen.width × scale` × `screen.height × scale`. To get coordinates from a position on the image, divide by scale.
- In a `--scale 1` image, pixels = points (402×874 on an iPhone 17 Pro in portrait). For an image shrunk with `--max-size` or the like, divide by the px/pt in stderr's `relay: image 471x1024 px, screen 402x874 pt, 1.172 px/pt (tap in points: px / 1.172)` (for example (120, 375) px → (102, 320) pt). With curl, response headers such as `X-Relay-Image-Scale` give the same values.
- Tap and swipe coordinates are always points, whichever image you use.
- Coordinates must be within `0 ≤ x < screen.width` and `0 ≤ y < screen.height` (displayed orientation). Outside, nothing is sent and you get 400 (exit code 7), with the screen size and orientation in a message like `invalid field: x, y: (500, 100) is outside the screen, which is 402x874 points in portrait (...)`. `swipe` checks both the start (`from:`) and the end (`to:`). Use points a few points inside the edge rather than right at it.
- `relay orientation` answers on every device once the screen has settled, so you can tap right after it: `{"ok":true,"requested":"landscapeLeft","orientation":"landscapeLeft","screenChanged":true}`. `requested` is the pose you asked for and `orientation` the screen's direction; use `orientation`. It waits up to 3 seconds for a change and 1 more for it to settle. An orientation the app doesn't support, or the current one, gives `screenChanged:false` after 3 seconds, still exit 0. Most iPhone apps don't rotate to `portraitUpsideDown`. Read `screen` in `relay health` again before building coordinates. An older runner answers only `{"ok":true}`: `relay stop`, then `relay start --rebuild`.
- In iPad windowed mode (windowed apps, Stage Manager, Slide Over), coordinates are still for the whole screen. The frames from `find` and `source` (except `--format text`) and the `point` of an element tap are converted to screen coordinates too, and `window` in the response (`x`, `y`, `width`, `height`, `scale`) gives the window's position and scale. Full-screen apps don't have it. If the position can't be determined, a tap sends nothing and exits 6 (`window_position_unknown`), while `find` and `source` keep frames in window coordinates, with `window: null` and a `warning` (a leading `warning …` line with `--format compact`).
- Inside the share sheet (`UIActivityViewController`), elements are reported relative to the sheet; relay adds the sheet's position, so their frames and element taps are in screen coordinates too. When it can't tell where they are, a tap sends nothing and exits 6 (`remote_view_position_unknown`), and `find` and `source` add a `warning`: tap by coordinates from a screenshot. On iPad, the sheet's elements aren't in the tree at all.
- See "座標系と画面の向き", "iPad のウィンドウ表示", and "表示が複数ある端末（iPhone Duo）" in docs/screen.md of device-relay.

## iPhone Duo

- On devices with several displays (iPhone Duo; Xcode 27.1 or later, iOS 27.1 or later), `displays` in `relay health` lists the displays (the outer is ID 1 at 466×678, the inner ID 3 at 669×951, in portrait points) and `display` is the ID of the foreground app's display. `screen`, screenshots (response header `X-Relay-Display`), coordinates, and element frames are for that display. The inner one is normally landscape, with a `screen` of 951×669.
- Fold the simulator with `relay hinge open` (180°), `close` (0°), `half` (90°), or an angle (`relay hinge 120`); `relay hinge` alone reads the angle. It answers once the hinge is at that angle, and at 0° and 180° once the display has switched, in about 2.5 seconds: `{"ok":true,"angle":94.6,"mechanicalAngle":90,"display":3}`. `mechanicalAngle` is the angle set; `angle` (the sensor's) can be a few degrees off. It is also a `relay run` step.
- Between the ends, which display is on depends on the direction: opening switched to the inner one between 160° and 165°, closing back between 20° and 15°. Use `display` from the answer, not the angle.
- A physical device or a simulator without a hinge: exit 6, `hinge_unavailable`, nothing sent. Angle or display not confirmed: exit 6, `hinge_failed`. An old runner: exit 8; if you started it, `relay stop`, then `relay start --rebuild`.
- On a Duo simulator, `relay orientation` sets the device pose. On the inner display the pose and the screen differ by 90°, so `requested` and `orientation` differ and that is not a failure: `portrait` → screen `landscapeRight`, `landscapeLeft` → `portrait`, `portraitUpsideDown` → `landscapeLeft`. `landscapeRight` would be upside down, so most apps stay as they are (`screenChanged:false`, still exit 0).
- After a fold or a rotation, read `relay health` again. When done, put the hinge back as you found it (`relay hinge close` for a closed Duo).
- Folding with Xcode's Device Hub could restart the simulator and stop the runner (`relay status`, then `relay start` again); `relay hinge` didn't.
- If the foreground app's display can't be determined (`display` is `null`, and touches and screenshots exit 6 with `cannot tell which display`), both displays are dark, so check with the user. On the inner display, when SpringBoard or an app that doesn't rotate is in front, `--mode xcui` coordinate touches are refused with `xcui touches can't reach`, so drop `--mode` and send it again.

Checked on an iPhone Duo simulator (iOS 27.2, Xcode 27.2) with the host app ([test-app](test-app.md)): folding, all four poses on both displays, and element and coordinate taps after each.
